# DEMO - SkillProof MX | Week14 |3 minutes +30 seconds

##0:00-0:35 | Open canonical /pricing
This week I added a pricing slice to SkillProof MX. The employer is the proposed payer; candidates continue to demonstrate skills for free. The pilot price is180 Mexican pesos per vacancy, for up to20 assessments over30 days. It is explicitly a price test, not an active purchase. Special customization requires a separate scope and quote.

##0:35-1:10 | Show segments and boundary
The first segment is small firms hiring occasionally; repeated hiring is a second unvalidated hypothesis. We charge by vacancy rather than assuming a monthly subscription. The employer's team reviews the evidence; SkillProof does not currently assign a human reviewer. The existing review button provides instructions, not a submitted request.

##1:10-2:10 | Simulator, save and reload
This simulator models the service's revenue, not the employer's savings. With100 eligible employers,10% conversion,one vacancy per paying employer per month and180 pesos per vacancy, gross revenue is1,800 monthly. Annualized revenue is21,600 only if the same month repeats. Including illustrative processing, infrastructure and human effort leaves200 contribution before fixed costs; that is not profit. I change frequency to0.25 to represent one vacancy every four months. I save in this browser, reload and load the scenario. The restored values and outputs appear. If I enter101% conversion, the output clears and saving is disabled.

##2:10-3:00 | /about and fix evidence
The memo compares the same20 candidates. Assumed costs fall from3,000 to1,380 pesos including price. The assumed time value divided by price is10:1; the total-cost improvement is only2.17 times. Neither is measured. The fresh-context synthetic persona confused human-cost assumptions with an included reviewer; I clarified ownership. A mechanical regression also exposed numeric strings accepted from saved JSON. I enforced numeric types, reran34 tests and redeployed.

##3:00-3:30 | What changed my mind
I started by assuming automation would save time. Now I see that setup, learning, review and corrections may add work, so I must measure the complete task before claiming savings. A polished calculator is not willingness to pay. My director's conditional interest supports a pilot, not a sale. My next real-world step is an independent retest with exact observations; an invited follow-up still does not count as a genuine voluntary return.
